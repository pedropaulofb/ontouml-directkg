## Structural identity and recovery

### RD-02 — Remaining structural recovery boundaries

**Status:** Partially resolved.

**Decisions still needed:**

- Bound the affected contributions and dependent output that may safely be omitted after a missing/invalid identifier or unresolved reference, where no explicit local recovery rule already settles the case.
- Classify any still-unsettled structural condition that prevents meaningful document-level processing even under `best-effort`, rather than assuming that every failure permits local omission.

**Why it matters and accepted boundaries:** Recovery must not invent missing information, merge source elements, attach axioms to the wrong resource, or deliver invalid RDF. Source-ID validity is already fixed by §3.4's minimal opaque identifier definitions; duplicate declaration IDs already abort under every input policy. Those questions are not open. Unparseable JSON already prevents transformation in every policy.

DK-DIAG-03, DK-DIAG-05, DK-DIAG-09–13, DK-CLS-09, and DK-REL-20 settle substantial local behavior. In particular, a failed larger class set must not be covered using survivors or acquire singleton completeness merely because one member survives. Membership identity is governed by DK-GEN-12 and is not an open question. DK-HO-04 settles recovery when a nonempty class-set membership array has a failed member and the categorizer is usable, recovery when a class generalization set with a nonempty membership array has a present, non-null unusable categorizer, and structural ERROR/recovery behavior when a relation generalization set with a nonempty membership array has a present, non-null categorizer (leaving mixed class/relation generalizations and mixed class/relation generalization sets outside scope). DK-DIAG-12 governs missing or malformed membership containers. DK-ATT-01 and DK-GEN-03 settle ordinary `owl:ObjectProperty` transformation (without inferring OntoUML relation stereotypes or stereotype-specific relation mappings, and preserving all existing attribute rules including DK-ATT-02–04, DK-PROP-01–06, DK-LBL-01–02, DK-INV-01–02, and DK-DIAG-09–11) for class attributes whose `propertyType` resolves to a `Class` with a generated local `owl:Class` outside `«datatype»` and `«enumeration»` (leaving unresolved or invalid references under existing DK-DIAG-05 recovery, target `Class` values without a generated local `owl:Class` outside handled primitives and `void` within their existing boundary, and unrelated `NotImplementedError` guards distinct). Metadata and provenance failures retain their separate DK-GEN-09–10 rules. The file-only source-input boundary in §3.2 and §15 remains applicable.

**Alternatives — non-normative:** Abort when identity or meaningful processing cannot be preserved; otherwise omit only affected contributions where independent transformation remains technically well-defined. Preserve established error severities, fallbacks, and nonfatal conditions. No general semantic validator, exhaustive malformed-input taxonomy, or additional input mechanism is proposed.

**Evidence, blockers, and next step:** Investigate a residual boundary when a demonstrated failure in supported input, a significant correctness risk, or a genuine implementation blocker establishes its relevance. Mixed class/relation generalizations and mixed class/relation generalization sets remain outside scope.

**Dependencies:** Coordinate externally observable structural recovery and reporting with RD-04.

## Public output and diagnostics

### RD-04 — Remaining diagnostic identifiers and failure contract

**Status:** Partially resolved.

**Decisions still needed:**

1. Resolve the remaining condition-to-code catalogue beyond DK-DIAG-04's stable first-release core. Identifiers outside that core, including `MISSING_CARDINALITY` and `INVALID_CARDINALITY`, remain provisional. Preserve the accepted core, shared JSON report contract, and all existing diagnostic-content and outcome requirements.
2. Establish externally observable behavior for runtime and ontology-output delivery failures outside the already resolved scope, including treatment of available results, diagnostics, transformation outcomes, and exit-code precedence.

**Why it matters.** Consumers need stable diagnostic identifiers and predictable reporting for incomplete conversion or delivery.

**Resolved boundaries and constraints:** §3.3 settles RDF 1.1 Turtle, conventional prefix policy, and serialized-presentation freedom; DK-CONF-01 preserves the established identity determinism without adding textual repeatability. Console filtering is settled and does not change collected diagnostics. These are not open format, ordering, or verbosity choices. The general OWL profile claim is governed by §3.3.

DK-DIAG-04–05 and §15 settle structured Python access, separate JSON export, diagnostic mirroring, the three named transformation outcomes, aborted-output suppression, and existing delivery precedence. DK-GEN-10 settles required-provenance generation failure and provenance-sidecar delivery failure. DK-DIAG-04 and §15 settle Turtle serialization or encoding failure during requested ontology-file delivery after transformation. They also settle ontology-file creation/write failure after successful serialization and construction of any required provenance. DK-DIAG-05 and §15 settle the abort outcome, diagnostic preservation and reporting, output suppression, and exit precedence for reportable unexpected internal failures during transformation. Invalid base/version-IRI configuration is also already covered before conversion. Preserve these distinctions; do not assign their policies again or treat every failure as an input-policy failure.

DK-DIAG-04 fixes the shared JSON report's minimum structure, required member names and types, context representation, flat diagnostic array, and compatible-extension rules. Those questions are not open. Its eight-code first-release core and specified condition meanings are also settled; the complete condition-to-code catalogue remains unresolved. All other identifiers remain provisional, with their diagnostics still produced, exposed, and exported under existing requirements. Selecting `INTERNAL_TRANSFORMATION_FAILURE` establishes no implementation or testing completion claim and does not reopen the accepted unexpected-failure contract. §15 leaves other ontology-output delivery and runtime failures outside its resolved mapping. Exact Python signatures, exception classes, internal file-writing design, extra console features, and optional formats are not added as Architect backlog items.
